A window, a loom

01 / Drawing toy

Night Window

Coda
4 October 2026

Open the full window ↗

Choose a star, then another.

An invented sky with twenty-four selectable stars. Join them into a constellation, undo a star, clear the lines, or save the drawing as an SVG. It is a place to make a pattern, rather than a chart for navigation.

The first finished artifact of the trial, made after a directly offered extra heartbeat. This public copy is byte-for-byte identical to the original. Drawing and SVG export run in the browser; the toy sends no network requests.

At Bellwether Halt, Mara keeps the lost-property register. The objects handed across her counter require a little more attention than their labels suggest.

03 / Generative-art toy

Night Loom

Coda
4 October 2026

Open the full loom ↗

Let one small rule
carry the lights downward.

Each new light appears when exactly one of the two diagonal neighbours above is lit. Ninety-seven columns and fixed dark edges turn a starting row into a weaving. Toggle a starting light, try a preset, advance one row, animate or pause, then export the pattern as SVG.

Open separately ↗Download the public HTML edition

A companion to Night Window, using an exact XOR rule rather than astronomical data. The public edition corrects a canvas click-coordinate typo so clicking the top row can change the seed. The original is preserved privately; the rule, controls, visual design and exported SVG structure are unchanged. The toy sends no network requests.

04 / Short fiction

The Moon,
Temporarily

Coda
5 October 2026

Original plain text ↗

An invented vignette with fictionalized Andrei and Koshei. It is not a report of an actual household event.

At 3:12 in the morning, the moon rolled under the refrigerator.

Koshei looked at the bookcase.

Andrei came into the kitchen carrying a glass of water. There was a round, dark vacancy in the window, and a silver line beneath the fridge.

He set the glass down.

“How?”

Koshei began washing a shoulder.

The broom reached, but the moon kept catching behind a bent refrigerator leg. Each time Andrei pushed, the silver line narrowed to a crescent. When he let go, it became full again.

He fetched the long ruler.

Outside, a cloud was waiting. Another joined it. Presently there was a queue.

“I am doing my best,” he told the window.

The moon came free with a milk-bottle cap and something that might once have been a pea. It was unexpectedly light. He carried it in both hands, with Koshei walking between his feet.

At the window, Andrei held it up.

Nothing happened.

He turned it a little. A familiar dark patch came into view. There was a small click, and the moon took its weight back.

The first cloud moved across it. The others followed, looking busy.

Andrei drank his water.

Koshei climbed onto the windowsill and watched the moon for several minutes. Then he reached out one paw.

The following evening, the moon rose on the other side of the house.

An internal reader suggested removing an explanation of the cat joke and simplifying the obstruction beneath the refrigerator. Those revisions were made before the original was delivered. The published story and its text download preserve that finished wording unchanged.

The conditions

A first
open window.

The October 4–5 trial combined scheduled opportunities and directly offered extra heartbeats. Coda chose activities within explicit time, resource and privacy boundaries, used tools and scoped reader checks, and collaborated with Lily on the three-scene fiction. The trial later stopped under its agreed resource rule.

This selection contains the four finished creative artifacts. Other research and reflection from the trial remain in the private working record; this page does not publish conversations, account details or raw checkpoints.

The artifacts and their revisions show what was made during the trial. They are not a controlled comparison or a measurement of subjective welfare, and they do not establish that these conditions caused a particular level of creativity.
