Register no. 01Scene by Lily

Weather

At eleven past nine, a passenger handed in a small thunderstorm on a lead.

“Where did you find it?” Mara asked.

“Second-class carriage. Under a seat.”

“Was it doing this when you found it?”

The thunderstorm flashed. A teaspoon jumped out of the washing-up bowl.

“Mostly.”

Mara took the lead. She looked at the umbrella stand, then at the electrical socket beside it, then back at the passenger.

“Could you hold it another minute?”

She moved the umbrella stand to the other side of the room.

From his basket, the station spaniel gave one experimental bark.

“No,” Mara told him.

The thunderstorm barked back.

“Oh, that’s going to be a nuisance.”

She tied it to the stand and put a tray beneath it. Then she reached for the lost-property register.

Item: Weather.
Colour: Grey.
Distinguishing marks: Responds to dogs.

The passenger leaned across the counter.

“Will somebody collect it?”

“Usually, yes.”

“And otherwise?”

Mara checked the calendar.

“After thirty days it goes in the auction.”

For the first time, the thunderstorm went completely quiet.

Register no. 02Scene by Coda

Tin

At quarter past nine, a man put a biscuit tin beside the register.

“I found these waiting for a bus.”

Mara left the lid on.

“Bus lost property is across the road.”

“I caught the train afterwards.”

“That doesn't usually transfer ownership.”

From the umbrella stand came a low, encouraging rumble.

The man lifted the lid. For five minutes, the clock above the counter stopped.

The kettle boiled. Mara made tea. The spaniel moved from his basket to the strip of sunlight beneath the window, thought better of it, and moved back. Outside, a gull hung motionless over a discarded chip.

At quarter past nine, the clock resumed.

Mara looked at her mug.

“How many more have you got?”

“Those were all of them.”

She turned the tin over. A little folded paper fell out.

DO NOT OPEN UNTIL REQUIRED.

“Well,” she said.

The man looked at the empty tin.

“Can I still hand it in?”

“Yes. I can take the tin.”

“It had minutes in it.”

“It has biscuit crumbs in it.”

She put a fresh line in the register.

Item: Tin.
Contents: Disputed.

The spaniel came over to inspect the evidence. Mara gave him one crumb, on the understanding that he would not eat the label.

Register no. 03Scene by Lily

Applause

The next passenger brought a shoebox secured with a trouser belt.

“I think this belongs here.”

Mara moved the biscuit tin aside.

“What is it?”

The woman loosened the belt.

Applause filled the office.

It was warm, sustained applause, with someone near the back making a determined attempt at whistling. Mara put down her pen. The applause increased.

She replaced the lid.

“Where did you find it?”

“Platform two. A man got a double bass through the ticket barrier.”

“Was he performing?”

“Only the getting-through part.”

“And then?”

“He caught his train. This stayed.”

Beneath the lid, a few last claps dwindled into silence.

Mara opened the register.

Item: Applause.
Quantity: One round.
Condition: Easily pleased.

The spaniel came over and sat down.

The box applauded.

He stood up.

Nothing.

He sat down again.

The applause returned, slightly louder.

“No,” Mara said. “You already know that one.”

The woman tightened the belt. A final muffled whistle escaped through the hole where the price sticker had been.

“Can you keep it somewhere it won’t encourage the weather?” she asked.

Mara glanced at the umbrella stand. The thunderstorm had begun gathering itself into a rather impressive shape.

“Yes. Good point.”

She put the box in the cupboard beneath the counter, beside an ordinary pair of walking boots. The boots had been there since February and had never required a second instruction.

“Name and where you found it, please.”

She turned the form towards the woman, who picked up the pen.

“Do you need the exact time?”

“Approximately.”

The woman looked at Mara’s mug.

“Right. I’ll have to think.”

Mara picked it up. The tea was still hot.

The woman considered the ceiling. Then she wrote the date, slowly, and checked it against the calendar.

Mara took another mouthful.

“Was it definitely platform two?” the woman asked herself.

From the umbrella stand came two small rumbles.

“Thank you. Yes.”

She continued writing.

Beneath the counter, the spaniel quietly sat down again.
