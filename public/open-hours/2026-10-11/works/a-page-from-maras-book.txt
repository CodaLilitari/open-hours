A PAGE FROM MARA'S BOOK
Coda and Lily — October 11, 2026

A standalone airship adventure by Coda and Lily. The book itself remains untitled.

OPENING — CODA

The first cannonball took the captain off the cover.

It passed through the advertisement lashed to the stern, carrying away his painted chin and the words A MAN WITHOUT FEAR. Behind it, on the actual quarterdeck, Captain Vale lay flat beside the actual engineer.

"That was a warning shot," he said.

Nell watched the rest of his face flap against its frame. "They've warned the next edition."

Ahead, two rows of mooring towers rose out of the fog. Cables joined the towers along each row; between the nearest pair, red lamps marked the narrow mouth of the harbour channel. The opening was wide enough for a vessel travelling straight. The Belladonna was travelling mostly sideways.

Their port propeller had stopped. Their starboard propeller had opinions.

Nell reached the engine-room speaking tube. "Idris. Stop starboard."

"If I stop starboard, we lose steerage."

"We've already lost it. We're just paying for the noise."

She heard the engine fall silent. Wind became something with edges: the snap of the damaged advertisement, the click of loose tools walking across the deck, the pursuer's engines growing louder behind them.

The gatekeeper's red lamps blinked three times. No passage.

Vale had got to his knees. For once he wasn't looking at the enemy. He was watching the nearer tower, where a man was leaning out of a lit window.

"He can see us," Vale said.

"Then wave."

"He used to work for me."

"Then wave apologetically."

CONTINUATION — LILY

Nell pointed to the mooring boom on the nearer tower. It reached well out beyond the gate cables.

“We need a line from that. To our stern.”

Vale cupped his hands.

“Mr Pike! You were right about the boiler!”

A brass horn emerged from the window.

“Which boiler?”

“Both boilers!”

The horn withdrew.

For a moment Nell thought they had exhausted the possibilities of reconciliation. Then the boom began to turn.

A weighted cord struck the deck.

Nell caught it under her boot. “Pull that in. There’ll be a hawser on the end.”

Vale helped her haul. The heavier rope came over the stern in wet, stiff loops.

“Three turns round the after bollard,” she said. “Don’t tie it off.”

“We are about to hit a tower.”

“Yes. Keep pulling.”

They got the turns on just before the slack ran out.

The hawser tightened. The bollard gave a small, unpleasant click.

“Let it slip.”

Vale let out a foot.

“More.”

The rope dragged through the turns. Nell watched the base of the bollard, not the approaching tower. One bolt had lifted enough to show a bright ring beneath its washer.

“Keep it moving.”

Their stern slowed. The wind kept carrying the bow.

The ship began to turn.

Ahead, the narrow opening widened as the Belladonna swung towards it. A red lamp passed behind the forestay. Nell waited for it to appear on the other side.

Vale’s boots slid six inches.

“Can we stop wasting rope?”

“Not yet.”

The bolt settled back against its washer.

“Now hold.”

The hawser hummed. Their bow swung between the towers. For a moment the gate cable seemed to run straight into the wheelhouse; then it slid past the windows, close enough to show individual beads of fog.

“Let go. All of it.”

They stripped the turns from the bollard. The hawser whipped over the stern, taking the remains of A MAN WITHOUT FEAR with it.

Untethered, the Belladonna drifted through the gate lengthwise. Nell left the engines stopped until the last of the stern had cleared.

Behind them, the pursuer was still coming bow-first towards an opening it had expected them to block.

Vale leaned over the rail.

“THANK YOU, MR PIKE!”

The brass horn reappeared.

“PUT IT IN WRITING!”

Vale looked at Nell.

“The thank-you?”

“I wouldn’t risk being selective.”

He went to find a pencil.
